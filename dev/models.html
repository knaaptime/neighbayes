    Supported Models¶

    Model suite¶

    A model class names a process (Aspatial, SLX, SAR, SEM, SDM, SDEM) and a family (Gaussian, Tobit, logit, probit, and the count families), plus Flow when the outcome is an origin–destination flow: SAR, SARLogit, SARNegBinFlow. The design is a set of arguments to the same class: time_col (formula mode) or T (matrix mode) makes it a panel; unit_effects (pair_effects for flows) is None, "fixed", or "random"; time_effects=True adds fixed time effects; dynamic=True adds the time lag ("restricted" for the restricted dynamic SDM); and separable=True pins \(\rho_w = -\rho_d \rho_o\) in a flow model. A combination the package does not implement raises when the model is constructed. Each cell below lists the processes implemented for that combination.

    Gaussian panels take fixed or random unit effects and have dynamic (lagged-dependent-variable) forms. Every spatial flow model has a separable form (separable=True) that pins \(\rho_w = -\rho_d \rho_o\). The other families include negative binomial, zero-inflated NB and hurdle NB count models (and Poisson for cross-sectional flows). Outside the table, Multilevel nests units in groups in larger groups, with a graph and a process at every level (multilevel models). The sections below list every class with its equation.

    Cross Sectional Models¶

    OLS¶

    \[y = X\beta + \epsilon\]

    SLX¶

    \[y = X\beta + WX\theta + \epsilon\]

    SAR¶

    \[y = \rho Wy + X\beta + \epsilon\]

    SEM¶

    \[y = X\beta + u, \quad u = \lambda Wu + \epsilon\]

    SDM¶

    \[y = \rho Wy + X\beta + WX\theta + \epsilon\]

    SDEM¶

    \[y = X\beta + WX\theta + u, \quad u = \lambda Wu + \epsilon\]

    Panel Models¶

    Each Gaussian class is a panel when time_col or T is given; the fixed-effects models below take unit_effects="fixed" and time_effects=True for two-way effects, and the random-effects models unit_effects="random".

    OLS panel¶

    \[y_{it} = x_{it}' \beta + a_i + \tau_t + \epsilon_{it}\]

    SAR panel¶

    \[y_{it} = \rho Wy_{it} + x_{it}' \beta + a_i + \tau_t + \epsilon_{it}\]

    SEM panel¶

    \[y_{it} = x_{it}' \beta + a_i + \tau_t + u_{it}, \quad u_{it} = \lambda Wu_{it} + \epsilon_{it}\]

    SDM panel¶

    \[y_{it} = \rho Wy_{it} + x_{it}' \beta + Wx_{it}' \theta + a_i + \tau_t + \epsilon_{it}\]

    SDEM panel¶

    \[y_{it} = x_{it}' \beta + Wx_{it}' \theta + a_i + \tau_t + u_{it}, \quad u_{it} = \lambda Wu_{it} + \epsilon_{it}\]

    SLX panel¶

    \[y_{it} = x_{it}' \beta + Wx_{it}' \theta + a_i + \tau_t + \epsilon_{it}\]

    OLS panel (Random Effects)¶

    \[y_{it} = x_{it}' \beta + \alpha_i + \tau_t + \epsilon_{it}, \quad \alpha_i \sim N(0, \sigma_\alpha^2)\]

    The random-effects Gibbs sampler (this and the spatial variants below) updates \(\sigma_\alpha\) by interweaving a centred step (\(\sigma_\alpha \mid \alpha\)) with a non-centred one (\(\sigma_\alpha \mid \alpha/\sigma_\alpha\), exact under the Gaussian likelihood). The centred step alone mixes at about 1% of draws when the unit effects are small against \(\sigma/\sqrt{T}\), the common many-units, few-periods spatial panel.

    SAR panel (Random Effects)¶

    \[y_{it} = \rho W y_{it} + x_{it}' \beta + \alpha_i + \tau_t + \epsilon_{it}, \quad \alpha_i \sim N(0, \sigma_\alpha^2)\]

    SEM panel (Random Effects)¶

    \[y_{it} = x_{it}' \beta + \alpha_i + \tau_t + u_{it}, \quad u_{it} = \lambda W u_{it} + \epsilon_{it}, \quad \alpha_i \sim N(0, \sigma_\alpha^2)\]

    SDEM panel (Random Effects)¶

    \[y_{it} = x_{it}' \beta + W x_{it}' \theta + \alpha_i + u_{it}, \quad u_{it} = \lambda W u_{it} + \epsilon_{it}, \quad \alpha_i \sim N(0, \sigma_\alpha^2)\]

    Multilevel Models¶

    Multilevel¶

    \[\theta_\ell = \rho_\ell W_\ell \theta_\ell + X_\ell \beta_\ell + \Delta_\ell \theta_{\ell+1} + \varepsilon_\ell, \quad \varepsilon_\ell \sim N(0, \sigma_\ell^2 I), \quad \ell = 0, \dots, L, \quad \theta_0 \equiv y\]

    Units (level 0) nest in groups (level 1), which nest in larger groups, up to level \(L\): schools in districts in states, tracts in counties in states. Each level has its own graph \(W_\ell\), covariates \(X_\ell\), innovation sd \(\sigma_\ell\) and process, and each level’s effect \(\theta_\ell\) enters the equation of the level below through \(\Delta_\ell\), which maps a row to its parent. A level’s process is one of the acronyms used everywhere else. In place of the lag ("SAR", the default), any level may carry an error process ("SEM"), \(\theta_\ell = X_\ell\beta_\ell + \Delta_\ell\theta_{\ell+1} + v_\ell\) with \(v_\ell = \lambda_\ell W_\ell v_\ell + \varepsilon_\ell\), or none (None), and any level may add the Durbin terms \(W_\ell X_\ell\) ("SDM", "SDEM", "SLX").

    Every graph spans all groups at its level, whatever their parent: a West Texas county neighbours New Mexico counties. Because each level’s effect passes through the filter of the level below, a shift at the top reaches the units through every filter beneath it. In the reduced form with a lag at every level,

    \[y = S_0\big(X_0\beta_0 + \varepsilon_0 + \Delta_0 S_1(X_1\beta_1 + \varepsilon_1 + \Delta_1 S_2(X_2\beta_2 + \varepsilon_2))\big), \qquad S_\ell = (I - \rho_\ell W_\ell)^{-1},\]

    so a Texas policy spreads into Oklahoma’s border districts and from there into their schools. With two levels this chain and an additive entry of the effects coincide, and the model nests the two-level hierarchical SAR models: Dong & Harris’s HSAR (a lag at the units, an error process with no covariates above), the dual spatial-error model of Wolf et al. (spvcm), and Lacombe & McIntyre’s upper-level lag or error with Durbin terms. The units carry the only intercept; upper levels drop theirs, since through row-standardized filters constants at different levels are confounded.

    from neighbayes.models import Level, Multilevel

m = Multilevel(
    Level("score ~ frl + log_enroll", data=schools, W=W_schools),         # level 0
    Level("~ income + segregation", data=districts, W=W_districts,
          key="district_id"),                                             # level 1
    Level("~ spending", data=states, W=W_states, key="fips",
          process="SEM"),                                                 # level 2
)
idata = m.fit()

    A level’s position is its index \(\ell\). key names the id column shared with the level below; it is looked up in the level below’s data, then in the units’ data, where strict nesting is checked. A Graph’s ids order a level’s groups (its data are reindexed to them). In matrix mode, groups gives each lower row’s parent label. The posterior follows the levels: rho_ℓ (or lam_ℓ for an error process), beta_ℓ, sigma_ℓ, and the effects theta_ℓ (coordinate group_ℓ).

    Priors. \(\sigma_0^2 \sim \text{Inv-}\Gamma(2, s_w^2)\), the data-informed prior of the single-level models with \(s_w^2\) the pooled variance of \(y\) within level-1 groups: \(\operatorname{Var} y\) would also count the upper levels’ variance and bias \(\sigma_0\) upward when those levels are strong. Each upper level’s \(\sigma_\ell \sim \text{half-}t_3(0, \operatorname{sd} y)\), since every level’s effects are in the outcome’s units; Gelman et al. (2008) priors on each level’s \(\beta_\ell\); uniform priors on each graph’s stability bounds for \(\rho_\ell\). See MultilevelPriors.

    Sampling. Given the \(\rho\)’s and \(\sigma\)’s, the coefficients of every level and all the effects are jointly Gaussian with a sparse precision, so the Gibbs sampler draws them in one sparse Cholesky block. Drawing them together removes the ridge between the units’ intercept and the mean of the effects. Every \(\rho_\ell\) and upper-level \(\sigma_\ell\) is drawn from its conditional with that block integrated out (parametrization="collapsed", the default), at one refactorization per evaluation. Three other schemes update the upper levels’ \(\rho_\ell\) and \(\sigma_\ell\) given the block instead:

    • "centred", given the effects;

    • "noncentred", given the standardized innovations \(\varepsilon_\ell/\sigma_\ell\), a move that needs no Jacobian;

    • "interweave", both in turn (Yu & Meng 2011).

    These need only the block’s conditional, so they carry over to non-Gaussian likelihoods.

    On a three-level lag model with 16 units per group, the collapsed scheme gave 7–14× the effective sample size of interweaving for the upper levels’ \(\rho\) and \(\sigma\), and the most per second.

    backend="jax", the default when JAX is installed, runs the same sweep compiled, using sparsax’s CHOLMOD and LU. When the top level is small (a few dozen states, say), its \(\rho\) and \(\sigma\) are updated through a dense Schur complement, which spares the large factorizations. The sweep is compiled once per model structure and reused by later fits, including fits to new data of the same shape, so a simulation study pays for compilation once. Per sweep it ran:

    • 2–10× faster than NumPy with about 600 units;

    • 1.5–4× faster with 14,400 units, where the collapsed scheme’s cost is CHOLMOD’s own factorization. sampler="nuts" fits the same model non-centred. Gibbs and NUTS agree across lag, error and no-process configurations. The units’ intercept is heavy-tailed: its spread grows as the top level’s \(\rho\) nears 1, where NUTS diverges.

    Impacts. impacts(level=ℓ) reports the impacts of level \(\ell\)’s covariates on the units, through the composed multiplier \(S_0\Delta_0 S_1 \cdots \Delta_{\ell-1}S_\ell\), where \(S_m\) is the identity for a level with an error process or none:

    • direct: the mean effect on a unit of a shift in its own group;

    • total: the mean effect of a shift in every group;

    • indirect: the difference, which is what reaches a unit from other groups through the filters.

    on="level" gives the impacts on \(\theta_\ell\) itself, through \(S_\ell\) alone. The own-group trace is exact up to exact_max groups and estimated with Rademacher probes above.

    • Multilevel: Gaussian, cross-sectional, \(L \ge 1\) nested levels. Panels, count likelihoods and crossed (non-nested) classifications are planned.

    Dynamic Panel Models¶

    Pass dynamic=True to a panel, or dynamic="restricted" to an SDM panel. Dynamic panels take no unit effects (Nickell 1981) but may take time_effects=True.

    OLS, dynamic (dynamic linear model)¶

    \[y_{it} = \phi y_{i,t-1} + x_{it}' \beta + a_i + \tau_t + \epsilon_{it}\]

    SDM, restricted dynamic (dynamic="restricted")¶

    \[y_{it} = \phi y_{i,t-1} + \rho W y_{it} - \rho \phi W y_{i,t-1} + x_{it}' \beta + W x_{it}' \theta + a_i + \tau_t + \epsilon_{it}\]

    SDM, dynamic (unrestricted)¶

    \[y_{it} = \phi y_{i,t-1} + \rho W y_{it} + \theta W y_{i,t-1} + x_{it}' \beta + W x_{it}' \theta + a_i + \tau_t + \epsilon_{it}\]

    SAR, dynamic¶

    \[y_{it} = \phi y_{i,t-1} + \rho W y_{it} + x_{it}' \beta + a_i + \tau_t + \epsilon_{it}\]

    SEM, dynamic¶

    \[y_{it} = \phi y_{i,t-1} + x_{it}' \beta + a_i + \tau_t + u_{it}, \quad u_{it} = \lambda W u_{it} + \epsilon_{it}\]

    SDEM, dynamic¶

    \[y_{it} = \phi y_{i,t-1} + x_{it}' \beta + W x_{it}' \theta + a_i + \tau_t + u_{it}, \quad u_{it} = \lambda W u_{it} + \epsilon_{it}\]

    SLX, dynamic¶

    \[y_{it} = \phi y_{i,t-1} + x_{it}' \beta + W x_{it}' \theta + a_i + \tau_t + \epsilon_{it}\]

    Non-Linear Models¶

    Binary models¶

    A binary outcome is the sign of a latent utility, \(y_i = \mathbf{1}[y_i^* > 0]\). The latent SAR propagates the link’s own disturbance through the multiplier, standard logistic for the logit and standard normal for the probit, so its \(\rho = 0\) member is the aspatial model and no scale parameter is pinned: the link fixes it. The reduced form propagates only the covariates. An unmarked SAR class is the form its field means by the name (ADR 0003): the latent SAR for the probit, SARProbit, and the reduced form for the logit, SARLogit; latent= names the other. The SEM and SDEM classes are latent in both links and take no latent argument.

    The latent models share one exact Gibbs sweep: the utilities from their truncated normal conditionals (Albert and Chib 1993), one at a time on NumPy or a colour class at a time on JAX (the default), then \(\rho\) or \(\lambda\) with \(\beta\) integrated out, then \(\beta\), and a scale move on \((y^*, \beta)\). The logit adds the Kolmogorov scales that make its disturbance a normal scale mixture (Holmes and Held 2006). The likelihood is an orthant probability, so there is no NUTS path; idata_kwargs={"log_likelihood": True} integrates each observation’s utility out of its likelihood, given the others’, for LOO. Impacts are on the latent index (scale="index" for the probit, "logodds" for the logit) or on \(P(y_i = 1)\) (scale="probability").

    SARProbit (latent SAR)¶

    \[y^* = (I - \rho W)^{-1}(X\beta + \varepsilon), \quad \varepsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)\]

    The textbook SAR probit (McMillen 1992; LeSage and Pace 2009, §10.1).

    SARProbit, reduced form (latent=False)¶

    \[y_i \sim \operatorname{Bernoulli}(\Phi(\eta_i)), \quad \eta = (I - \rho W)^{-1} X\beta\]

    Sampled as the reduced SARLogit is, with Albert–Chib utilities in place of its Pólya–Gamma step: \(\beta\) conjugate given the utilities, \(\rho\) by a slice with \(\beta\) integrated out, where the log-determinant cancels.

    SEMProbit¶

    \[y^* = X\beta + u, \quad u = \lambda W u + \varepsilon, \quad \varepsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)\]

    SDEMProbit¶

    \[y^* = X\beta + WX\theta + u, \quad u = \lambda W u + \varepsilon, \quad \varepsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)\]

    SEMProbit with random unit effects (unit_effects="random")¶

    \[y_j = \mathbf{1}[x_j'\beta + c_{u(j)} + \varepsilon_j > 0], \quad c = \lambda W c + v, \quad v \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_c^2 I), \quad \varepsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)\]

    Each unit, a row of \(W\), is observed more than once (unit_col, or units in matrix mode, gives each observation’s unit), and the error process carries one random effect per unit (unit_effect, with sd unit_effect_sd) in place of the probit’s disturbance: LeSage and Pace’s (2009, §10.6) spatially structured effects probit (Smith and LeSage 2004). It is fitted by NUTS; its probability impacts are the average marginal effects, with no indirect part.

    Tobit (SAR Tobit)¶

    \[y_i = \max(c, y_i^*), \quad y^* = \rho W y^* + X\beta + \varepsilon, \quad \varepsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2 I)\]

    Tobit (SEM Tobit)¶

    \[y_i = \max(c, y_i^*), \quad y^* = X\beta + u, \quad u = \lambda Wu + \varepsilon, \quad \varepsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2 I)\]

    Tobit (SDM Tobit)¶

    \[y_i = \max(c, y_i^*), \quad y^* = \rho W y^* + X\beta + WX\theta + \varepsilon, \quad \varepsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2 I)\]

    Panel Tobit (SAR)¶

    \[y_{it} = \max(c, y_{it}^*), \quad y_t^* = \rho W y_t^* + X_t\beta + \varepsilon_t\]

    Panel Tobit (SEM)¶

    \[y_{it} = \max(c, y_{it}^*), \quad y_t^* = X_t\beta + u_t, \quad u_t = \lambda W u_t + \varepsilon_t\]

    SARTobit and SEMTobit given time (time_col, or T) fit the pooled panel. It takes no unit or time effects, because a within transformation would mix censored and uncensored values in the likelihood. Impacts are on the latent scale, through the spatial multiplier within each period.

    Panel count models (SARNegBin, NegBin)¶

    \[y_{it} \sim \operatorname{NegBin}(\mu_{it}, \alpha), \quad \log \boldsymbol{\mu}_t = (I - \rho W)^{-1} (X_t\beta + c) + \tau_t\]

    Unit effects \(c\) come from unit_effects="random" and period effects \(\tau_t\) from time_effects=True. The Gaussian panels’ within transform does not carry to a log link, so the effects are parameters, held as a unit index rather than dummy columns. The unit effects enter inside the spatial filter, with the covariates, as random effects do in every spatial equation of the package: persistent unobservables propagate as \(X\) does. Under the partial pooling below this is a different model from independent effects added after the filter, not a reparameterization of it: the propagated effects \((I - \rho W)^{-1} c\) carry the SAR covariance \(\sigma^2 (A'A)^{-1}\), so the effects themselves inform \(\rho\). The two coincide only for unpooled effects. The Gibbs sampler integrates the unit effects out of every \(\rho\) update by an ω-weighted analogue of the within transform, recomputed each sweep from the Pólya–Gamma weights; its effect block has the sparse precision \(\operatorname{diag}(w) + A'A/\sigma^2\), factored by CHOLMOD at each candidate \(\rho\).

    The unit effects are partially pooled: \(c_i \sim N(\mu, \sigma^2)\) with \(\sigma\) learned (a half-\(t_3\) prior with scale unit_effect_sd_scale, default 1; the posterior’s unit_effect and unit_effect_sd, or pair_effect and pair_effect_sd in the flow panels), so a unit seen in few periods is shrunk toward the common mean in proportion to how little its data say. Spatial panels typically have many units and few periods (Elhorst 2014), and there a fixed, wide prior leaves each unit’s level pinned only by its own few periods, which biases the dispersion \(\alpha\) and \(\rho\) (the incidental-parameter problem) and lets \(\rho\) drift toward 1, where the filter’s amplification of an unanchored level goes unchecked. The unit effects absorb no column: the intercept and time-invariant covariates stay in the design. Period effects keep a fixed prior and absorb the columns that vary only over time. Pooling assumes the effects are unrelated to \(X\); when they are not, mundlak=True adds the unit means of the time-varying columns to the design inside the filter (Mundlak 1978), which absorbs the correlation (in a nonlinear model only approximately). The Gibbs sampler updates \(\sigma\) by interweaving a centred step (\(\sigma \mid c\)) with a non-centred one (\(\sigma \mid \tilde c\), \(\tilde c = (c - \mu)/\sigma\); Yu & Meng 2011), the latter on the exact likelihood rather than the Pólya–Gamma working one, so no augmentation layer stands between \(\sigma\) and the data (Papaspiliopoulos, Roberts & Sermaidis 2011). That keeps \(\sigma\) mixing in the large-\(N\), small-\(T\) regime, where each unit’s data say little about its effect. Both classes default to Gibbs; sampler="nuts" runs the PyMC model. priors={"alpha_fixed": a} holds \(\alpha\) fixed; a large value (10-20 times the typical mean count) gives an essentially Poisson model that keeps the exact Pólya–Gamma sampler.

    SARNegBin (Reduced Form)¶

    \[y_i \sim \operatorname{NegBin}(\mu_i, \alpha), \quad \mu = \exp(\eta), \quad \eta = (I - \rho W)^{-1} X\beta\]

    No disturbance propagates — spatial dependence enters only through the mean propagator. Supports both NUTS and Pólya–Gamma Gibbs sampling. Unit effects need a panel (above): in a cross-section one draw per observation is a disturbance, not a unit effect.

    SARNegBin, latent (latent=True)¶

    \[y_i \sim \operatorname{Poisson}(e^{\eta_i}), \quad (I - \rho W)\eta = X\beta + \varepsilon, \quad \varepsilon_i = \log\gamma_i,\ \gamma_i \sim \operatorname{Gamma}(\alpha, \alpha)\]

    The latent SAR: the multiplier propagates the negative binomial’s own gamma disturbance, so \(\rho = 0\) is NegBin and the GLM suite’s LM-Lag is its score test. The Gibbs sampler carries the log-means \(\eta\), given which the disturbances are a sparse linear map and every conditional is log-concave: the log-means are drawn one at a time by slice on NumPy and a colour class at a time by independence Metropolis–Hastings on JAX (the default), then \(\rho\) with \(\beta\) shifted along the least-squares fit of \(W\eta\) on \(X\), \(\beta\) from a t at its Laplace approximation, and \(\alpha\). Small counts pin \(\eta\) only loosely, so two moves interweave the parametrization that holds the disturbances instead (Yu & Meng 2011): \(\alpha\) with the standardized disturbances held, and \(\rho\) with the disturbances held. There is no NUTS path. The pointwise log-likelihood (idata_kwargs={"log_likelihood": True}) integrates each observation’s latent value out given the others, \(p(y_i \mid \theta, \eta_{-i}) = \int p(y_i \mid \eta_i)\, p(\eta_i \mid \eta_{-i}, \theta)\, d\eta_i\), a one-dimensional integral over the equations the column of \(I - \rho W\) touches. That is the integrated importance sampling of Li, Qiu, Zhang and Feng (2016), whose weights PSIS-LOO handles; the likelihood conditional on \(\eta_i\) would give degenerate ones (Merkle, Furr and Rabe-Hesketh 2019). It is comparable with the reduced form’s: both estimate \(\sum_i \log p(y_i \mid y_{-i})\).

    As a panel (T), it takes random unit effects and period effects, both inside the filter as in the Gaussian SAR with random effects, \((I - \rho W)\eta_t = X_t\beta + \tau_t + c + \varepsilon_t\), \(c_i \sim N(\mu, \sigma_c^2)\) with \(\sigma_c\) learned; each period’s gamma disturbance propagates through the same filter, so the Jacobian is \(T\log|I - \rho W|\). The sweep runs over the stacked periods, and a unit effect, entering only its own unit’s equations, is drawn from a t at its conditional mode.

    Impacts are on the log-mean by default; impacts(scale="count") gives them on the expected count, which averages over the propagated heterogeneity. With \(u = S(\rho)\varepsilon\) and \(m = S(\rho)X\beta\),

    \[\mathbb{E}\,y_i = e^{m_i}\prod_j \frac{\Gamma(\alpha + S_{ij})}{\Gamma(\alpha)\,\alpha^{S_{ij}}}, \qquad \frac{\partial\,\mathbb{E}\,y_i}{\partial x_{jk}} = \mathbb{E}\,y_i\,S_{ij}\beta_k,\]

    the reduced form’s count-scale impact with each row scaled by the mean of the propagated heterogeneity. The product needs every entry of \(S\), so each posterior draw estimates it by Monte Carlo over fresh disturbances (one batched sparse solve), taking the unit’s own factor in closed form where the multiplier’s diagonal is exact. The same holds, with \(S\) replaced by \((I - \lambda W)^{-1}\) and no spillover through the mean, for SEMNegBin and SDEMNegBin.

    SEMNegBin¶

    \[y_i \sim \operatorname{Poisson}(e^{\eta_i}), \quad \eta = X\beta + (I - \lambda W)^{-1}\varepsilon, \quad \varepsilon_i = \log\gamma_i,\ \gamma_i \sim \operatorname{Gamma}(\alpha, \alpha)\]

    The latent SEM, sampled as the latent SAR is, with \(\beta\) shifted so that the disturbances keep their level as \(\lambda\) moves; \(\lambda = 0\) is NegBin and the GLM suite’s LM-Error is its score test. It takes no latent switch.

    SDEMNegBin¶

    \[y_i \sim \operatorname{Poisson}(e^{\eta_i}), \quad \eta = X\beta + WX\theta + (I - \lambda W)^{-1}\varepsilon\]

    SEMNegBin on the design \([X, WX]\). Impacts are on the log-mean, direct \(\beta\) and indirect \(\theta\) (times the mean row sum of \(W\)), or with scale="count" on the expected count.

    SARZINB¶

    \[y_i \sim \operatorname{ZINB}(\mu_i, \alpha, \pi_i), \quad \log \boldsymbol{\mu} = (I - \rho W)^{-1} X\beta, \quad \operatorname{logit} \boldsymbol{\pi} = (I - \rho_z W_z)^{-1} X_z\beta_z\]

    Zero-inflated negative binomial: a spatial-lag logit zero part (is the unit active?) and a spatial-lag NB count part, both reduced-form SARs. The zero part’s parameters are the count part’s names with a _zero suffix: rho_zero and beta_zero in the posterior, X_zero, formula_zero and W_zero in the constructor, and beta_mu_zero, rho_lower_zero and so on in the priors. Pólya–Gamma Gibbs by default; sampler="nuts" fits the same model in PyMC. Activation probabilities, zero attribution and fitted means are posterior expectations; posterior_predictive simulates the zero process too.

    Identification. Only the shape of the count distribution separates structural zeros from sampling zeros. When active units average about one count, NB with a smaller α explains the zeros nearly as well as zero inflation does. The zero part is then weakly identified, the posterior of rho_zero is close to its prior, and the Gibbs chain mixes slowly. Repeated periods fix this: in a panel with unit (or pair) effects, a unit’s other periods pin down its count distribution, and the zeros in excess of it are structural. For “any versus none” questions on sparse counts, use a hurdle model (below), whose zero part is fit to the observed zeros.

    SARZINB, latent (latent=True)¶

    \[y_i = 0 \text{ if } u_i \le 0, \quad y_i \mid u_i > 0 \sim \operatorname{Poisson}(e^{\eta_i}), \quad (I - \rho_z W_z)u = X_z\beta_z + \varepsilon_z, \quad (I - \rho W)\eta = X\beta + \varepsilon\]

    Each part is a latent SAR with its own disturbance: the zero part is the latent SAR logit (\(\varepsilon_z\) logistic; a unit is active when its utility is positive), the count part the latent SAR negative binomial (\(\varepsilon_i = \log\gamma_i\), \(\gamma_i \sim \operatorname{Gamma}(\alpha, \alpha)\)). The sweep is the two latent samplers in turn, coupled through the zero counts: a zero leaves its unit’s activation unknown, so its utility’s side of zero is drawn given the count part, the active side weighted by \(P(y_i = 0 \mid \text{active}) = e^{-e^{\eta_i}}\), and the count part then observes the active units only. zero_attribution is the sampler’s Rao–Blackwellized probability that each zero is structural. log_likelihood integrates both latent values out of each observation, for LOO. impacts(equation, scale=...) reports each part on its link scale (the default) or its outcome scale: "probability" of activation, and "count", the expected count of an active unit. The identification caveat above applies unchanged. As a panel (\(T\) periods stacked time-first, each filter acting within a period) the count part takes random unit effects inside its filter and period effects (unit_effects="random", time_effects=True), as the latent SARNegBin panel does; the zero part takes none, as in the reduced form. NumPy or backend="jax".

    ZINB panels (SARZINB, ZINB)¶

    The same two parts per period, \(T\) periods stacked time-first, with per-period structural zeros and unit and period effects on the count part (unit_effects="random", time_effects=True, as for the NB panels). The zero part’s design X_zero may vary over time.

    Flow ZINB (SARZINBFlow, separable)¶

    Both parts are separable flow models, \(\eta_z = (L_{z,o} \otimes L_{z,d})^{-1} X_z\beta_z\) and \(\eta^{\mathrm{cnt}} = (L^{\rho}_o \otimes L^{\rho}_d)^{-1} X\beta\), sampled by a structured \(n \times n\) sweep (NumPy or backend="jax") that never forms an \(n^2 \times n^2\) matrix. The panel adds pair and period effects on the count part; the cross-section is the panel with \(T = 1\). Flow counts are usually sparse, so the identification caveat above bites hardest here: use the panel with pair effects, or the flow hurdle.

    Hurdle models (SARHurdleNegBin and family)¶

    \[P(y > 0) = \operatorname{logit}^{-1}(\eta_z), \quad y \mid y > 0 \sim \operatorname{NB}(\mu, \alpha) \text{ truncated at } 0, \quad \eta_z = (I - \rho_z W_z)^{-1} X_z\beta_z, \quad \log \boldsymbol{\mu} = (I - \rho W)^{-1} X\beta\]

    A hurdle separates whether a count is positive from how large it is when it is. Both parts are reduced-form SARs: the spatial lag acts on the linear predictor and no latent disturbance enters, so neither part has a Jacobian. The zero part is named as in SARZINB (rho_zero, beta_zero, X_zero, W_zero).

    Hurdle or ZINB. Both give positive counts the same zero-truncated NB distribution; they differ only in the probability of a positive count. The hurdle models it directly, \(P(y > 0) = \pi\). ZINB splits it into an activation probability and the NB’s own chance of a positive count, \(\pi\,(1 - \operatorname{NB}(0))\), and so can call a zero structural. The data see that split only through the shape of the positive counts, which on sparse counts barely constrains it (see SARZINB). The hurdle’s zero part is fit to the observed zeros, so it is identified however sparse the counts are. What it gives up is the split: its beta_zero and rho_zero describe whether any count occurs, not whether a unit is “open”. Use the hurdle when the question is “any versus none”, and ZINB, in a panel with unit or pair effects, when the question is “structurally closed versus open but quiet”.

    Sampling. The zero part is a Pólya–Gamma logit. In the count part the truncation is augmented exactly: each positive cell gets a geometric count of the zeros the truncation hid, after which the cell is Pólya–Gamma conjugate. When the positive counts are mostly ones, the count level and α trade off along a ridge, so they are drawn jointly by a slice sampler that moves along it. sampler="nuts" fits the same model in PyMC (HurdleNegativeBinomial).

    • SARHurdleNegBin: cross-section, with separate X_zero (or formula_zero) and W_zero for the zero part.

    • Panels (SARHurdleNegBin, HurdleNegBin with time_col or T): \(T\) periods stacked time-first, with unit and period effects (unit_effects="random", time_effects=True) in both parts. In the zero part a unit effect absorbs the unit’s baseline chance of a positive count, so beta_zero comes mostly from within-unit switches between zero and positive periods. The posterior names the zero part’s effects unit_effect_zero, unit_effect_sd_zero and time_effect_zero. Both parts’ unit effects are partially pooled, with their own learned sds; the pooling is what keeps α and ρ unbiased when units have few positive periods (about three per unit in simulations, where a fixed, wide prior gave α at half its true value).

    • Flows (SARHurdleNegBinFlow(separable=True), cross-section or panel): both parts are separable flow models, sampled by the structured \(n \times n\) sweep (NumPy or backend="jax"), with pair and period effects in both parts.

    The halves share no parameters, so the posterior factorizes. Linking them through correlated unit (pair) effects is a planned extension.

    Latent (SARHurdleNegBin(latent=True)). Each part is a latent SAR with its own disturbance: a count is positive when the utility \(u = (I - \rho_z W_z)^{-1}(X_z\beta_z + \varepsilon_z)\), \(\varepsilon_z\) logistic, is positive, and is then \(\operatorname{Poisson}(e^{\eta_i})\) truncated at zero, \(\eta = (I - \rho W)^{-1}(X\beta + \varepsilon)\), \(\varepsilon_i = \log\gamma_i\). The truncation conditions on \(\eta_i\), as in hurdle models with random effects. The parts still share nothing: the zero part is SARLogit(latent=True) fitted to \(\mathbf 1(y > 0)\), the count part the latent SAR NB observing the positive counts only, and a sweep is one sweep of each. LOO and impacts work as for the latent SARZINB, the count part’s outcome being the expected positive count. As a panel both parts take random unit effects and period effects, each inside its own filter; given the logistic’s normal scales the zero part’s unit effects are Gaussian. NumPy or backend="jax".

    Logit¶

    \[y_i \sim \operatorname{Bernoulli}(p_i), \quad \operatorname{logit}(p) = X\beta\]

    Non-spatial logistic regression baseline.

    NegBin¶

    \[y_i \sim \operatorname{NegBin}(\mu_i, \alpha), \quad \log \boldsymbol{\mu} = X\beta\]

    Non-spatial negative binomial baseline.

    SARLogit (Reduced Form)¶

    \[y_i \sim \operatorname{Bernoulli}(p_i), \quad \eta = (I - \rho W)^{-1}X\beta\]

    The reduced form: the spatial multiplier acts on the mean of the log-odds. Pólya–Gamma Gibbs sampler only — no NUTS path. Binary outcomes pin \(\rho\) slowly: expect to need about 900 units or more.

    There is no spatial logit with random effects. In a cross-section the effects’ sd trades off against \(\beta\) (the logit’s attenuation) and against \(\rho\) or \(\lambda\), and binary outcomes barely constrain the trade: on 1,225 units with effects of sd 2, an SEM logit with such effects had its \(\lambda\) near 0.3 against 0.5 and the effects’ sd at 6 ± 6, held only by its prior.

    SARLogit, latent (latent=True)¶

    \[y_i = \mathbf 1\{y^*_i > 0\}, \quad (I - \rho W) y^* = X\beta + \varepsilon, \quad \varepsilon_i \sim \operatorname{Logistic}(0, 1)\]

    The latent SAR: the multiplier propagates the logit’s own disturbance, so \(\rho = 0\) is the logit and the GLM suite’s LM-Lag is its score test. It has no variance parameter, which is why it is identified where the random-effects logits were not. The Gibbs sampler is exact: it draws the latent utilities (Albert and Chib 1993), the Kolmogorov scales that make the logistic a normal scale mixture (Holmes and Held 2006, through augax), \(\rho\) with \(\beta\) integrated out, then \(\beta\), and ends with a scale move that rescales the utilities and \(\beta\) together (Liu and Wu 1999), which keeps \(\rho\) mixing as it nears one (at \(\rho = 0.97\) it quadruples its effective draws). It runs on JAX by default, drawing the utilities a colour class at a time (classes of utilities that share no row of \(I - \rho W\) are conditionally independent), and on NumPy one at a time. There is no NUTS path (the likelihood is an orthant probability). Its pointwise log-likelihood integrates each utility out as the negative binomial’s does, the observation’s term being the probability that the utility falls on the observed side of zero.

    Impacts are on the latent index by default; impacts(scale="probability") gives them on \(P(y_i = 1) = P(u_i > -m_i)\), with \(u = S(\rho)\varepsilon\) and \(m = S(\rho)X\beta\): \(\partial P(y_i = 1)/\partial x_{jk} = f_{u_i}(-m_i)\,S_{ij}\beta_k\), the logistic counterpart of the SAR probit’s \(\phi\) term in LeSage and Pace (2009). The density of the weighted sum of logistics \(f_{u_i}\) is estimated per posterior draw by Monte Carlo, conditioning on every disturbance but the unit’s own, whose logistic density then enters exactly. SEMLogit and SDEMLogit take the same scale.

    SEMLogit¶

    \[y_i = \mathbf 1\{y^*_i > 0\}, \quad y^* = X\beta + (I - \lambda W)^{-1}\varepsilon, \quad \varepsilon_i \sim \operatorname{Logistic}(0, 1)\]

    The latent SEM, sampled as the latent SAR is; \(\lambda = 0\) is the logit and the GLM suite’s LM-Error is its score test. A SEM whose error process carried no disturbance would be the aspatial logit, so the SEM logit takes no latent switch.

    SDEMLogit¶

    \[y_i = \mathbf 1\{y^*_i > 0\}, \quad y^* = X\beta + WX\theta + (I - \lambda W)^{-1}\varepsilon, \quad \varepsilon_i \sim \operatorname{Logistic}(0, 1)\]

    SEMLogit on the design \([X, WX]\), sampled the same way: \(\theta\) carries the covariates’ spillover and \(\lambda\) the clustering of the disturbance, which a latent SAR ties together through one \(\rho\). Impacts are on the latent index, direct \(\beta\) and indirect \(\theta\) (times the mean row sum of \(W\)), or with scale="probability" on the probability.

    SARPoissonFlow¶

    \[y_{ij} \sim \operatorname{Poisson}(\mu_{ij}), \quad \log \boldsymbol{\mu} = A(\boldsymbol{\rho})^{-1} X\beta\]

    No dispersion parameter. Sampled by auxiliary-mixture Gibbs (Frühwirth-Schnatter & Wagner 2006) rather than Pólya–Gamma, which admits no exact Poisson representation. Use it for counts close to Poisson: the normal mixture behind the sampler has lighter tails than the error it replaces, so under overdispersion it is biased toward large counts (fit reports the Pearson dispersion and warns above 2). For overdispersed counts use the NB flow models; an NB with priors={"alpha_fixed": a}, a 10-20 times the typical mean, is an essentially Poisson model on the exact Pólya–Gamma sampler.

    The recommended Poisson flow model — the separable restriction removes the weakly-identified \(\rho\) ridge of the unrestricted variant.

    Panel Flow Models¶

    Stack the flow models above across \(T\) periods in time-first order; any flow class is a panel when T is given, with pair_effects and time_effects. The NB panels take pair and period effects as parameters, the pair effects partially pooled, \(\log \boldsymbol{\mu}_t = A(\boldsymbol{\rho})^{-1} (X_t\beta + c) + \tau_t\), with one effect per origin-destination pair held as an index, never as \(n^2\) dummy columns; see the panel count models above. The separable panels (NB, ZINB, hurdle) keep their structured \(n \times n\) sweep. Their \(n^2 \times n^2\) effect block cannot be integrated out at scale, so the sweep holds the effects while \(\rho\) moves (interweaving steps with the effects and with their propagated values held), draws them by conjugate gradients, and moves the time-invariant coefficients (intercept, distance, intraunit indicators) jointly with the effects they trade off against.

    Specification Tests¶

    Lagrange-multiplier tests for choosing a spatial specification, called directly from neighbayes.diagnostics.lmtests or through spatial_diagnostics() and spatial_diagnostics_decision() on any fitted model.

    Each Gaussian test is the score test of the general nesting model, \((I - \rho W)y = X\beta + WX\theta + u\), \((I - \lambda W)u = \varepsilon\), at the fitted null. The score of the tested parameters is evaluated at every posterior draw, Neyman-orthogonal to everything the null estimates (Doğan, Taşpınar & Bera 2021),

    \[g^*_T = g_T - J_{TF}J_{FF}^{-1}g_F,\]

    with the efficient information \(J_{TT} - J_{TF}J_{FF}^{-1}J_{FT}\) at the posterior mean. At a draw the scores \(g_F\) of the null’s own parameters are not zero, as they are at the restricted maximum, so a raw score correlated with them would carry their posterior spread: the lag score does, through the part of \(WX\beta\) in the span of \(X\) (for a row-standardized \(W\), the intercept’s \(\beta_0 W\mathbf 1 = \beta_0\mathbf 1\)). The reported statistic and p-value are the test at the posterior-mean parameters, the counterpart of the score test at the estimate; the per-draw values give the posterior distribution behind the credible interval. A robust test also removes the directions its alternative leaves at zero (Bera & Yoon 1993), so a test robust only to parameters the null estimates is the plain test.

    The last but one row is ambiguous by construction: without a spillover term to compare it against, an error term alone cannot say whether one \(\rho\) moved it.

    The GLM scores are orthogonalized and the statistic taken at the posterior-mean parameters as for the Gaussian tests.

    Origin–destination flows¶

    The flow tests are the score tests of the lag-flow model \(y = \rho_d W_d y + \rho_o W_o y + \rho_w W_w y + X\beta + \varepsilon\) at a fitted OLSFlow, cross-section or panel (the panel on the Lee and Yu transformed likelihood), built as the Gaussian tests are. With \(W_d = I_n \otimes W\), \(W_o = W \otimes I_n\) and \(W_w = W \otimes W\), every trace in the information factors into traces of \(W\), so the \(n^2\) pairs never form a Kronecker product.

    Sampling Backends¶

    Choosing a sampler¶

    fit() takes sampler={"gibbs", "nuts", None}, and None — the default — selects Gibbs whenever the model has a registered Gibbs sampler, and NUTS otherwise. For SAR/SEM/SDM/SDEM, the count families, the ZINB model and the Gaussian panel families, that means Gibbs unless you ask for something else.

    NUTS is not universally available. SARLogit (in both forms), SEMLogit, SDEMLogit, the latent SARNegBin, SEMNegBin, SDEMNegBin and the auxiliary-mixture Poisson flow classes build no PyMC graph at all, and fit(sampler="nuts") raises NotImplementedError. Robust (Student-t) SAR/SEM/SDM/SDEM and Gaussian panel FE models keep Gibbs: the Student-t error is sampled as a normal scale mixture with the same fixed nu the NUTS path uses. Robust random-effects and Tobit models still require NUTS.

    target_accept is NUTS-only and raises TypeError if passed with Gibbs.

    Execution backends¶

    For any Gibbs sampler, backend selects the execution path:

    Value

    Behaviour

    "auto"

    default — JAX when installed and supported by the family, else NumPy

    "jax"

    the sweep compiled into one XLA program; chains run in parallel, vectorized under jax.vmap for the Gaussian families and one thread per chain for the others

    "numpy"

    pure NumPy/SciPy; chains as separate processes via joblib, controlled by n_jobs

    Both backends implement the same sampler and target the same posterior. With sampler="nuts", backend names the NUTS implementation instead: "pymc" (the default), "nutpie", "numpyro" or "blackjax". A backend that belongs to the other sampler raises.

    A JAX sweep is compiled once per model structure (its dimensions and its process, effect and prior options) and reused for the rest of the session. The first fit pays for compilation, from under a second to a few seconds; a refit, a longer run, or new data of the same dimensions on the same graph does not, so a simulation study compiles once rather than once per replicate.

    Log-Determinant Methods¶

    The log-determinant \(\log|I - \rho W|\) is evaluated at every MCMC draw, and is the term that makes large problems expensive. Two arguments of the model constructor (not of fit()) choose how it is computed, and both samplers honour them:

    • logdet_method is the method: "eigenvalue" sums over the eigenvalues of \(W\) from one dense eigendecomposition; "cheb", "aaa" and "spline" interpolate values from a sparse factorization (Chebyshev, AAA rational, cubic spline), exact to the interpolant’s tolerance; "stochastic" is a stochastic Chebyshev expansion (Han et al. 2015) that needs no factorization and is the one method with sampling error.

    • logdet_decomp is the factorization an interpolating method uses: "chol" (sparse Cholesky through scikit-sparse’s CHOLMOD, for an undirected \(W\)) or "lu" (sparse LU by KLU or UMFPACK, routed by the sparsity pattern, for any \(W\)). "auto" takes "chol" where the method has it and \(W\) is undirected, else "lu"; "eigenvalue" and "stochastic" ignore it.

    Fill-in guard. Size alone does not predict factorization cost — a dense or hub-dominated graph blows up under Cholesky regardless of \(n\). Before committing to a factorization the selector estimates \(\mathrm{nnz}(W^2)/\mathrm{nnz}(W)\) in \(O(\mathrm{nnz})\); if that exceeds 20 it warns and falls back to stochastic. A KNN-50 graph or a fully dense \(W\) at moderate \(n\) takes that branch.

    Adaptation. With logdet_adapt=True (the default) the Gibbs sampler fits the log-determinant to where the chains are halfway through warmup: an interpolant is refit on that ρ range (recorded in idata.attrs["logdet_refit_window"]), AAA adds factorizations near a singularity, and stochastic grows its probe pool until the bias it leaves is small against the posterior sd.

    Flows. The unrestricted three-ρ flow models take "trace_series" (exact, from trace moments of the \(n \times n\) \(W\)) or "resolvent" (a stochastic resolvent estimator whose accuracy improves with the flow sample size, run on fit(backend=...)); None takes the trace series for an undirected \(W\) and the resolvent for a directed one. The separable flows factor the log-determinant into \(n \times n\) terms and take the single-\(W\) methods above.

    Cutoffs are configurable through the environment: NEIGHBAYES_LOGDET_EIGEN_MAX_N (default 500), NEIGHBAYES_LOGDET_FACTOR_MAX_N (default 60000), and NEIGHBAYES_LOGDET_MAX_FILLIN_RATIO (default 20).
